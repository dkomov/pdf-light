# From a 9 MB limit to PDF Light

PDF Light started with an ordinary design problem: a finished PDF needed to fit below a 9 MB upload limit without sacrificing its appearance. The designer was using pixel snapping, had not manually converted fonts to outlines, and wanted to understand what could be improved during layout rather than simply accept a compressed file.

The investigation moved beyond the usual advice to shrink images. In the inspected documents, verbose vector content was an important source of weight. The PDF contained coordinate data at a much finer precision than its final appearance required. Pixel snapping in an editor did not prevent the exporter from generating long decimal values after transformations and unit conversion.

An early assumption about outlined fonts was corrected by the designer. That distinction matters: a PDF's internal representation is not evidence of a particular action in the source design file. The resulting implementation works on recognized PDF path operands, leaves text alone, and checks the extracted text and page appearance afterwards.

A second issue appeared in native macOS viewers: large artwork on the first and last pages could fail to display as expected. Comparing renderers pointed to a redundant opaque soft mask. Removing the narrowly identified mask fixed the native rendering while the independent rendering remained visually unchanged. This became a separate, conservative compatibility repair, rather than a general command to flatten every transparency effect.

For one 27-page report, the prototype reduced about 12.01 MB to about 8.39 MB. That is an observation about one document, not a benchmark or a promise about other PDFs. The report is private and is not part of this repository. Public screenshots instead use original synthetic artwork generated by `scripts/make_demo.py`.

The next question was practical: could this investigation become a small Mac app that other people could use? The prototype acquired drag and drop, original/result previews, target-size feedback, cancellation, a separate Save a copy action, and checks using both PDFium and macOS rendering. The public version adds seven languages, a standalone public-dependency build, tests, and documentation.

The project was developed by dkomov with assistance from OpenAI Codex. Its history records the prototype import and subsequent changes; it does not invent a long development history. The purpose of publishing under MIT is to make the implementation inspectable, useful, and improvable. Corrections, especially around PDF edge cases and translation quality, are welcome.

## Design lessons

- Inspect what contributes bytes in the exported PDF before changing the layout.
- Do not assume raster images dominate every document.
- Pixel snapping is useful for design alignment, but it is not a PDF compression setting.
- Preserve an editable design source and an untouched export; produce separate distribution copies.
- Compare the result in the viewer the recipient will use, not just one PDF engine.
- Treat screen distribution and print production as different requirements.
